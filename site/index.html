The answer in one picture

The headline hides a swap of customers

Net product revenue went from £9.16M to £9.33M (+1.9%). Customers who bought in both years spent 7.7% less (−£548k). Customers lost after Year A took £1.02M with them; new customers brought £1.40M, and sales without a customer ID grew by £341k. The year grew by replacing customers, not by growing them.

Q1
Growth

Where did revenue growth come from?

Not from the core market: the United Kingdom moved −£4,472. The rise came from Australia (+£108k) and France (+£64k), while EIRE fell (−£91k). No single product explains it.

Q2
Customers

How dependent is revenue on a few customers?

Heavily. The top 1% of customers (43 accounts) brought 30.2% of identified Year B revenue; the top 10 customers alone 16.7%. Among new customers, 38.8% ordered again within 90 days.

What this does not say

Concentration is measured on customers with an ID; 15.0% of Year B revenue has none and sits outside it. “New” means first seen in this data. A large account is not a risk by itself; the report shows the dependency, not its cost.

Q3
Cancellations

How much revenue is lost to cancellations?

3.1% of Year B product sales were cancelled, up from 2.6% in Year A. The rise is not a trend: 7 of the 10 largest single cancellation lines fall in Year B, and without those ten lines the rate went from 2.5% to 2.1%.

Can each cancellation be traced to an order?

Same customer, product and price on an earlier sale line.

93%6%

92.9% of cancelled value (85.7% of lines) matches an earlier sale. 6.2% has a customer but no matching sale; 0.9% has no customer ID. The traceable share of lines is 79.2% in Year A and 92.5% in Year B, consistent with orders placed before the data starts.

What this does not say

A cancellation is not necessarily lost margin or a refund: the data has no cost or payment information. Very large lines look like order-entry corrections, but the data cannot confirm that.

Limits

Assumptions and limitations

Read these before acting on any number above.

Two years of one shop, ending in 2011.

The data stops on 9 December 2011 and covers a single retailer. It describes these customers; it is not a benchmark for the market or for the business today.

Revenue only, no cost.

There is no cost or margin column, so nothing here says which products or customers are profitable.

Missing customer IDs are not random.

15.0% of Year B revenue (£1.40M) has no customer ID, and it grew 32% year on year. Who those buyers are cannot be told from this data.

History starts in December 2009.

A customer who is “new” here may have bought before. Cohorts before March 2010 are therefore left out of the repeat rates.

Duplicates were set aside, not deleted.

11,812 exact duplicate lines (£54k) are excluded. Some may be real repeat scans; the ledger keeps them visible so the choice can be reversed.

Cancellations are not refunds.

There is no payment data. “Traceable” means an earlier order exists for the same customer, product and price, not that it covered the whole cancelled quantity.
